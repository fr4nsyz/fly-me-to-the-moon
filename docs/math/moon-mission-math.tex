\section{Vectors}
For $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and scalar $s$:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} &= a_x b_x + a_y b_y + a_z b_z \\
\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} &= (a_y b_z - a_z b_y,\; a_z b_x - a_x b_z,\; a_x b_y - a_y b_x) \\
\lVert\mathbf{a}\rVert &= \sqrt{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}}
\end{align}
The cross product follows the right-hand rule: $\hat{x}\times\hat{y}=\hat{z}$.
Code: \texttt{vec3.hpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_vec3.cpp}.

\section{Time}
Mission time $t$ is seconds since the epoch
$\mathrm{JD}_0 = 2\,434\,743.5$ (1954-01-01 00:00 UTC; Bart Howard's original
``Fly Me to the Moon'' sheet music was published in 1954). Julian centuries from J2000:
\begin{equation}
T = \frac{(\mathrm{JD}_0 - 2\,451\,545.0) + t/86400}{36\,525}
\end{equation}
The subtraction is evaluated first. $\mathrm{JD}_0 - 2\,451\,545.0 = -16\,801.5$ is exact
in double precision, whereas adding $t/86400$ to $\mathrm{JD}_0$ first rounds time to the
spacing of doubles near $2.4\times10^6$, about $4\times10^{-5}$ s, which made the
differenced Moon velocity noisy at the $4\times10^{-2}$ m/s level.

\section{Moon ephemeris}
Circular orbit of radius $a_M$. Mean elements, Meeus,
\emph{Astronomical Algorithms}, ch.~47, linear terms (higher-order terms change the
result by about $0.0003^\circ$ at the epoch):
\begin{align}
L' &= 218.3164477^\circ + 481\,267.88123421^\circ\,T &&\text{mean longitude}\\
\Omega &= 125.0445479^\circ - 1934.1362891^\circ\,T &&\text{ascending node}\\
u &= L' - \Omega &&\text{argument of latitude}
\end{align}
Position in the ecliptic frame:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{ecl}} = a_M\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\Omega\cos u - \sin\Omega\sin u\cos i_M\\
\sin\Omega\cos u + \cos\Omega\sin u\cos i_M\\
\sin u\,\sin i_M\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
Rotation into the Earth-equatorial frame about $x$ by $\varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_M = \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\cos\varepsilon&-\sin\varepsilon\\
0&\sin\varepsilon&\cos\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{ecl}}
\end{equation}
The orbit's tilt $I$ to Earth's equator follows from
\begin{equation}
\cos I = \cos\varepsilon\cos i_M - \sin\varepsilon\sin i_M\cos\Omega
\end{equation}
At the epoch, $I = 25.988^\circ$, within the real range
$18.3^\circ$--$28.6^\circ$ over the 18.6-year nodal cycle.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The real orbit is eccentric ($e\approx0.055$), so the modeled
position differs from the true one by up to about $6^\circ$ in longitude and
$20\,000$ km in distance. $u = L'-\Omega$ is the standard approximation.
Code: \texttt{moon\_ephemeris.hpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_moon\_ephemeris.cpp}.

\subsection{Moon velocity}
Central difference with $h = 1$ s:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_M(t) \approx \frac{\mathbf{r}_M(t+h) - \mathbf{r}_M(t-h)}{2h}
\end{equation}
A smaller $h$ amplifies rounding in $\mathbf{r}_M$; a larger $h$ increases the
truncation error from the path's curvature. Measured: speed $1023.18$ m/s, matching the
mean orbital speed $a_M\,\dot L'$; $\mathbf{r}_M\cdot\mathbf{v}_M$ vanishes to
$3\times10^{-11}$ of $\lVert\mathbf{r}_M\rVert\lVert\mathbf{v}_M\rVert$; the result
differs from an $h = 0.5$ s difference by at most $5.2\times10^{-4}$ m/s over 30 days,
limited by rounding of the mean longitude angle (about $3900$ rad at the epoch).

\section{Gravity}
With $\mathbf{r}$ the spacecraft position, $\mathbf{r}_M$ the Moon position, and
$r=\lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert$, $r_M=\lVert\mathbf{r}_M\rVert$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = -\mu_E\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^3}
 + \mu_M\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}_M-\mathbf{r}}{\lVert\mathbf{r}_M-\mathbf{r}\rVert^3}
 - \frac{\mathbf{r}_M}{r_M^3}\right)
\end{equation}
The first term is Earth's pull. The Moon contribution is the third-body perturbation:
the Moon's pull on the spacecraft minus the Moon's pull on Earth. The subtracted part,
the indirect term, is required because the frame is centered on Earth, which itself
accelerates toward the Moon. Near Earth the two Moon terms nearly cancel; near the Moon
the first dominates.
Code: \texttt{dynamics.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_dynamics.cpp}.

\section{Engine and actuator limits}
The flight software commands a throttle $\tau$ and a direction $\mathbf{d}$. The plant
applies the limits of a real engine:
\begin{equation}
\tau' = \begin{cases} 1 & \tau > 1\\ \tau & 0 < \tau \le 1\\ 0 & \text{otherwise (including NaN)}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The engine is off ($\tau'=0$) when $\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert$ is zero, infinite, or NaN,
or when the mass has reached the dry mass $m_{\mathrm{dry}}$. Otherwise:
\begin{align}
F &= \tau' F_{\max} \\
\mathbf{a}_T &= \frac{F}{m}\,\frac{\mathbf{d}}{\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert} \\
\dot m &= -\frac{F}{I_{sp}\,g_0}
\end{align}
The direction is normalized, so a corrupted command can point the engine anywhere but
cannot push harder than full thrust. These limits are physics of the plant, not a
flight software defense.

\section{Equations of motion}
\begin{align}
\dot{\mathbf{r}} &= \mathbf{v} \\
\dot{\mathbf{v}} &= \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}, t) + \mathbf{a}_T \\
\dot m &= -\frac{F}{I_{sp}\,g_0}
\end{align}

\section{Numerical integration (RK4)}
Write the state as $\mathbf{y} = (\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}, m)$ and the equations of
motion as $\dot{\mathbf{y}} = f(\mathbf{y}, t)$. The classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta
step of size $h$:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{k}_1 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n,\, t_n) \\
\mathbf{k}_2 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_1,\, t_n + \tfrac{h}{2}) \\
\mathbf{k}_3 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_2,\, t_n + \tfrac{h}{2}) \\
\mathbf{k}_4 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + h\,\mathbf{k}_3,\, t_n + h) \\
\mathbf{y}_{n+1} &= \mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{6}\left(\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4\right)
\end{align}
The engine command is held constant for the whole step, as flight software sets one
command per tick. If $m_{n+1} < m_{\mathrm{dry}}$, it is set to $m_{\mathrm{dry}}$; the
step still applied thrust for its full length, so the error is at most one step of thrust.

\paragraph{Validation.} With no thrust, specific orbital energy
$\mathcal{E} = \tfrac12\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert^2 - \mu_E/r$ and angular momentum
$\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{v}$ are conserved. Over one 400 km circular orbit
in 5000 steps, measured drift is $2.5\times10^{-9}$ in $\mathcal{E}$ and
$1.3\times10^{-9}$ in $\lVert\mathbf{h}\rVert$, with the Moon's tidal pull present; the
spacecraft returns within 3 m of its start. Forward Euler on the same orbit drifts
$1.5\times10^{-2}$ in $\mathcal{E}$ and returns 510 km off.
Code: \texttt{integrator.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_integrator.cpp}.
