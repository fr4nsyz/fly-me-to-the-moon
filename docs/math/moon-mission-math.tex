\section{Conventions}
SI units throughout: meters, seconds, kilograms, radians.
Frame: origin at the Moon's center, $z$ along the Moon's spin axis, axes fixed relative
to the stars (Moon-centered inertial). The Moon does not move.
Source: \texttt{workloads/moon/common/}.

\section{Vectors}
For $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and scalar $s$:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} &= a_x b_x + a_y b_y + a_z b_z \\
\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} &= (a_y b_z - a_z b_y,\; a_z b_x - a_x b_z,\; a_x b_y - a_y b_x) \\
\lVert\mathbf{a}\rVert &= \sqrt{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}}
\end{align}
The cross product follows the right-hand rule: $\hat{x}\times\hat{y}=\hat{z}$.
Code: \texttt{vec3.hpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_vec3.cpp}.

\section{Gravity}
With $r = \lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = -\mu_M\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^3}
\end{equation}
At the surface, $\mu_M / R_M^2 = 1.6242$ m/s$^2$. Earth's tidal acceleration near the
Moon is on the order of $10^{-5}$ m/s$^2$ (estimated) and is not modeled.
Code: \texttt{dynamics.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_dynamics.cpp}.

\section{Engine and actuator limits}
The flight software commands a throttle $\tau$ and a direction $\mathbf{d}$. The plant
applies the limits of a real engine:
\begin{equation}
\tau' = \begin{cases} 1 & \tau > 1\\ \tau & 0 < \tau \le 1\\ 0 & \text{otherwise (including NaN)}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The engine is off ($\tau'=0$) when $\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert$ is zero, infinite, or NaN,
or when the mass has reached the dry mass $m_{\mathrm{dry}}$. Otherwise:
\begin{align}
F &= \tau' F_{\max} \\
\mathbf{a}_T &= \frac{F}{m}\,\frac{\mathbf{d}}{\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert} \\
\dot m &= -\frac{F}{I_{sp}\,g_0}
\end{align}
The direction is normalized, so a corrupted command can point the engine anywhere but
cannot push harder than full thrust. These limits are physics of the plant, not a
flight software defense.

\section{Equations of motion}
\begin{align}
\dot{\mathbf{r}} &= \mathbf{v} \\
\dot{\mathbf{v}} &= \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) + \mathbf{a}_T \\
\dot m &= -\frac{F}{I_{sp}\,g_0}
\end{align}
The system does not depend on time explicitly.
Code: \texttt{dynamics.cpp}, function \texttt{derivative}.

\section{Numerical integration (RK4)}
Write the state as $\mathbf{y} = (\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}, m)$ and the equations of
motion as $\dot{\mathbf{y}} = f(\mathbf{y})$. The classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta
step of size $h$:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{k}_1 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n) \\
\mathbf{k}_2 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_1) \\
\mathbf{k}_3 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_2) \\
\mathbf{k}_4 &= f(\mathbf{y}_n + h\,\mathbf{k}_3) \\
\mathbf{y}_{n+1} &= \mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{6}\left(\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4\right)
\end{align}
The engine command is held constant for the whole step, as flight software sets one
command per tick. If $m_{n+1} < m_{\mathrm{dry}}$, it is set to $m_{\mathrm{dry}}$; the
step still applied thrust for its full length, so the error is at most one step of thrust.

\paragraph{Validation.} With no thrust, specific orbital energy
$\mathcal{E} = \tfrac12\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert^2 - \mu_M/r$ and angular momentum
$\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{v}$ are conserved. Over one 100 km circular lunar
orbit (period 7067.5 s) in 5000 steps, measured drift is $2.1\times10^{-15}$ in
$\mathcal{E}$ and $8.9\times10^{-16}$ in $\lVert\mathbf{h}\rVert$, at the level of double
rounding, and the spacecraft returns to within a millimeter of its start. Forward Euler
on the same orbit drifts $1.5\times10^{-2}$ in $\mathcal{E}$ and returns 138 km off.
Code: \texttt{integrator.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_integrator.cpp}.

\section{Navigation (flight software)}
The flight software receives the Moon-centered state $(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})$ and
derives the ground-relative state:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{u} &= \mathbf{v} - \boldsymbol{\omega}_M \times \mathbf{r} &&\text{velocity relative to the ground}\\
\hat{\mathbf{n}} &= \mathbf{r} / \lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert &&\text{local up}\\
h &= \lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert - R_M &&\text{altitude}\\
v_{\uparrow} &= \mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}} &&\text{vertical speed, positive up}\\
v_{\leftrightarrow} &= \lVert\mathbf{u} - v_{\uparrow}\hat{\mathbf{n}}\rVert &&\text{horizontal speed}
\end{align}
This stage runs before guidance, so a fault here reaches every TMR replica.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The real lunar spin axis is tilted about $1.5^\circ$ from the
orbit normal (Cassini's laws); the model fixes it along $z$. Altitude is above a sphere
of radius $R_M$; there is no terrain.
Code: \texttt{fsw/navigation.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_navigation.cpp}.

\section{Guidance (flight software)}
Inputs: the navigation state and the mass $m$. Output: throttle $\tau$ and direction
$\mathbf{d}$; the plant applies the actuator limits.

\paragraph{Braking.} Full throttle, $\tau = 1$, split between altitude control and
horizontal deceleration. With $a_{\max} = F_{\max}/m$, $w = v_{\leftrightarrow}$,
$r = R_M + h$, and $h_b = 1500$ m:
\begin{align}
t_{\mathrm{stop}} &= w / a_{\max}\\
v^{*}_b &= -\frac{h - h_b}{\max(t_{\mathrm{stop}},\ 1\ \mathrm{s})}\\
a_{\uparrow} &= \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{\mu_M}{r^2} - \frac{w^2}{r} + k_v\,(v^{*}_b - v_{\uparrow}),\ 0,\ a_{\max}\right)\\
a_{\leftrightarrow} &= \sqrt{(a_{\max} - a_{\uparrow})(a_{\max} + a_{\uparrow})}\\
\mathbf{d} &= a_{\uparrow}\hat{\mathbf{n}} - \frac{a_{\leftrightarrow}}{w}\,(\mathbf{u} - v_{\uparrow}\hat{\mathbf{n}})
\end{align}
The sink-rate target brings the lander to $h_b$ as its horizontal speed reaches zero.
$w^2/r$ is the support still provided by orbital motion. The factored form of
$a_{\leftrightarrow}$ cannot go negative by rounding, because the clamp guarantees
$a_{\uparrow} \le a_{\max}$. Pure retrograde braking from 15 km (no altitude control)
was measured to strike the surface at 132 m/s with 726 m/s of horizontal speed remaining.

\paragraph{Switch.} Braking ends, permanently, when $v_{\leftrightarrow} < 50$ m/s or
$h < 500$ m, whichever comes first.

\paragraph{Final descent.} Target vertical speed and wanted acceleration:
\begin{align}
v^{*} &= -\,\mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{h}{10\ \mathrm{s}},\ 1,\ 30\right)\ \text{m/s}\\
g &= \frac{\mu_M}{(R_M + h)^2}\\
\mathbf{a}^{*} &= \left(g + k_v\,(v^{*} - v_{\uparrow})\right)\hat{\mathbf{n}}
 - k_h\,(\mathbf{u} - v_{\uparrow}\hat{\mathbf{n}})
\end{align}
with $k_v = 1.0\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $k_h = 0.5\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$. If
$\mathbf{a}^{*}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}} \le 0$ the engine is off (a lander cannot thrust toward
the ground). Otherwise
\begin{equation}
\tau = \frac{\lVert\mathbf{a}^{*}\rVert\, m}{F_{\max}}, \qquad \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{a}^{*}
\end{equation}
and the plant saturates $\tau$ at 1. On the target descent rate with no drift,
$\tau = g\,m/F_{\max}$, the hover throttle.

\paragraph{Clean landing.} Measured: touchdown at 527.8 s at 1.000 m/s descent and
0.000 m/s horizontal speed, with 1157.6 kg of propellant left; score 0.328. Two clean runs
produce byte-identical traces.
Code: \texttt{fsw/guidance.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_guidance.cpp}.

\section{Simulation and landing judge}
\paragraph{Start.} Circular equatorial prograde orbit at $h_0 = 15$ km:
$\mathbf{r}_0 = (R_M + h_0, 0, 0)$, $\mathbf{v}_0 = (0, \sqrt{\mu_M / (R_M + h_0)}, 0)$,
$m_0 = 15\,200$ kg. The flight software and the plant step together at $\Delta t = 0.1$ s,
for at most 3600 s.

\paragraph{Independence.} The judge computes altitude and ground-relative velocity with
the same formulas as navigation, in uninstrumented plant code. If it reused the flight
software's navigation, a fault there would corrupt the judge as well.

\paragraph{Touchdown.} When altitude changes sign within a step, from $h_a > 0$ to
$h_b \le 0$, the crossing fraction is $f = h_a / (h_a - h_b)$, and touchdown speeds,
time, and position are interpolated linearly at $f$.

\paragraph{Ground-fixed spot.} With $\alpha = -\omega_M t$, the touchdown point is rotated
back to the ground's orientation at $t = 0$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha & -\sin\alpha & 0\\ \sin\alpha & \cos\alpha & 0\\ 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{r}
\end{equation}
so two runs landing on the same ground spot at different times give the same $\mathbf{s}$.

\paragraph{Score.} With descent speed $v$, horizontal speed $w$, and distance $d$ from the
clean run's spot (zero for the clean run itself):
\begin{equation}
v_{\lim}(w) = 3.05 - (3.05 - 2.13)\frac{w}{1.22}, \qquad
S = \max\left(\frac{v}{v_{\lim}(w)},\ \frac{w}{1.22},\ \frac{d}{1000}\right)
\end{equation}
in m/s and m (ADR-0020). $S \le 1$ passes. No touchdown within the time limit gives
$S = \infty$. The plant reports $S$ as the run's final result.
Code: \texttt{plant/judge.cpp}, \texttt{sim/main.cpp}. Tests: \texttt{test\_judge.cpp}.
